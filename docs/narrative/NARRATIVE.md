## Narrative guardrails

`DERIVED` — PRD §4, §33; Brand Foundation §9; plus the constraint stated by PXTO.

The narrative must never make PXTO sound like:

1. a generic software house
2. a traditional web agency
3. an AI-only company
4. a low-code automation agency
5. a personal freelancer brand

Each has a characteristic voice, a characteristic structure, and a characteristic
failure. **§17 defines the diagnostic test for all five.** Every section below is
written to stay outside them; §17 is how you check.

## 1. Core narrative

`INTERPRETED` — from PRD §13, §57; Positioning §3.2, §14.

### 1.1 The through-line

> Companies do not lack technology. They lack the connections between it.
>
> Tools were bought one at a time, each solving its own problem. Nobody owned
> the space between them — so the operation filled that space with people.
> Re-typing what one system already knows. Spreadsheets that exist only to move
> data. A process that runs because someone remembers it.
>
> That work is invisible. It never appears as a line item. It only appears as a
> team that is always busy and an operation that cannot grow without adding
> people.
>
> PXTO works in that space. We start from the problem, not the technology, and
> then connect, automate, build or publish — whatever closing the gap actually
> requires.

### 1.2 The narrative in one line

Internal compass, not copy:

> **PXTO resolve o problema que está entre os sistemas.**

### 1.3 Why this is the through-line

| Requirement | How the narrative satisfies it |
| --- | --- |
| Communicate that PXTO understands **real business problems** | The story opens inside the reader's operation, not inside PXTO |
| Communicate **connect / automate / build** | All three appear as responses to one condition, not as a service menu |
| Hold four capabilities as peers (§4) | None is the story; the *gap* is the story |
| Survive a change of technology (§57) | No technology is named anywhere in it |
| Work without accumulated proof (Positioning §12) | It makes no claim about PXTO's history — only about where PXTO works |
| Avoid the five anti-patterns (§17) | It is neither a service list, nor a craft pitch, nor a technology pitch, nor a person |

### 1.4 The narrative's centre of gravity

Every page inherits one rule from this: **the reader's problem is the subject of
the sentence, and PXTO is the predicate.** Not "PXTO oferece integrações", but
"seus sistemas não conversam — e isso tem solução".

## 2. Main customer tension

`INTERPRETED` — from PRD §8.2, §13; Positioning §3, §4, §5.

Narrative requires tension. Without it, the site is a catalogue.

### 2.1 The central tension

> **"Já investimos em tecnologia. Então por que ainda fazemos tanta coisa na
> mão?"**

The reader has done the responsible thing. They bought the CRM, the ERP, the
tools. And the operation is still held together by people doing mechanical work.

This is the tension because it is **contradictory and unresolved in the reader's
own mind**. They cannot explain it, so they have absorbed it.

### 2.2 The tension decomposed

| Element | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **What they want** | An operation that runs without being carried |
| **What they have** | Tools that each work, and a whole that does not |
| **The obstacle** | The space between systems — which no product covers and no vendor owns |
| **Why unresolved** | It is chronic, not acute. Never urgent enough to prioritise |
| **What it costs** | Time, errors, capacity — all invisible, because absorbed by people |
| **What they fear** | Another vendor, another project, another thing that does not quite work |

### 2.3 The secondary tension — and why it shapes the site

> **"I know what is wrong. I do not know what to ask for."**

The buyer can describe the symptom precisely and the solution not at all. They
cannot write a brief, so they do not start.

This has a direct structural consequence, and it is one of the most important
in this document:

**The site must not require the reader to self-diagnose into a service
category.** A visitor who must decide whether their problem is "integration" or
"automation" before they can proceed will leave. Entry is through the *problem*;
the capability is the answer PXTO gives, not the question the visitor must
answer (Positioning §14.7, Brand Foundation §14.7).

### 2.4 The resolution the narrative offers

Not "buy our service". The resolution is:

> **You do not have to specify the solution. You have to describe the problem.
> That is where the work starts anyway.**

This is a genuine differentiator (Positioning §11.1.3), it is available to a new
company with no track record, and it directly relieves the secondary tension.

## 3. Problem narrative

`DERIVED` / `INTERPRETED` — PRD §13; Positioning §3, §5.

### 3.1 The PRD's framing

> **Sua empresa já tem tecnologia. O problema é quando ela não conversa.**

This line is strong for a specific reason worth preserving in any refinement:
**it does not blame the reader.** It does not say they chose badly. It says the
situation is structural.

### 3.2 The three narrative moves

The problem section must execute three moves, in order. Skipping any one breaks
the section.

**Move 1 — Recognition.** *"That is my company."*
Concrete, recognisable situations, not abstractions (§13): disconnected systems,
manual processes, duplicated data, scattered information, repetitive tasks, tools
that do not fit.
The test: could the reader have written this sentence about their own week?

**Move 2 — Naming.** *"So that is what this is."*
The six symptoms are named as one condition: the operation outgrew the tools it
assembled (Positioning §3.2). The reader gains a frame for something they had
only experienced as friction.
This move is what makes PXTO credible. Diagnosis precedes any claim of capability.

**Move 3 — Reframe.** *"This is fixable, and it is not my fault."*
The problem is structural, not a consequence of bad decisions — and it has a
resolution. Without this move the section is merely uncomfortable.

### 3.3 What the problem narrative must not do

- Not exaggerate into crisis language. The reader has lived with this for years;
  alarm reads as manipulation
- Not blame the reader's past choices or their existing vendors
- Not quantify the cost — no invented statistics about wasted hours (§53,
  Positioning §13.4)
- Not resolve into a service list. Move 3 opens the door; §4 walks through it
- Not use "transformação digital" or equivalent empty framing (§7)

### 3.4 Where the problem narrative appears

Primary: Home → Problema (§11.2). Also, in reduced form, at the opening of each
solution page and as the *Contexto/Problema* blocks of every project page (§21).
**The problem is always the entry point** — this is the site's structural
signature.

## 4. PXTO solution narrative

`INTERPRETED` — PRD §14, §15, §57; Positioning §7, §14.

### 4.1 The shape of the answer

The answer to "my systems do not talk" is **not** "we do integrations". That
answer sells a deliverable. The narrative answer is:

> **We start by understanding what is actually happening. Then we build what
> closes the gap — a connection, an automated flow, an application, a
> functioning digital presence. Which one it is, is an outcome of the diagnosis,
> not a decision you have to make first.**

### 4.2 Method as the primary evidence

`INTERPRETED` — this is the load-bearing idea of the solution narrative.

A new company cannot prove capability with volume (Positioning §12). What it can
show immediately is **how it works** — and PRD §15 provides exactly that:

```text
01 Entendemos → 02 Desenhamos → 03 Construímos → 04 Integramos → 05 Evoluímos
```

Narratively, this sequence does four things at once:

1. Proves the problem comes first — *Entendemos* precedes *Construímos*
2. Relieves the secondary tension (§2.3) — the reader does not need a spec
3. Answers the fear of abandonment after delivery — *Evoluímos*
4. Demonstrates rigour without claiming history

**Constraint:** the PRD forbids stating that every project passes through all
five steps (§15). Narrate it as how PXTO thinks, not as a fixed pipeline.

### 4.3 The four capabilities as one story

`DERIVED` — §14, §57.

| | Its role in the story |
| --- | --- |
| **Integrações** | Closes the gap *between* systems |
| **Automação** | Removes the manual work that lives *inside* the gap |
| **Software** | Builds what was missing from the gap entirely |
| **Sites** | The operational surface where the company meets the outside |

They are four answers to one condition — not four business lines. The narrative
must make the reader feel they are looking at **one company with four
instruments**, never at four departments.

### 4.4 How technology is narrated

`DERIVED` — §25, §57.

Technology is named as **an outcome of the problem**, never as the reason to be
interested. The PRD is explicit for Software: "o foco deve ser na solução e não
nas tecnologias utilizadas" (§25).

Narrative rule:

> Name a technology only where naming it makes the *solution* clearer. If it is
> there to impress, remove it.

### 4.5 AI inside the solution narrative

`DERIVED` — §4, §24, §57; Brand Foundation §14.3; Positioning §15.1.

AI appears exactly where the PRD puts it: as one of the tools inside the
proposition. Concretely:

- **Never** in the hero, navigation, main message or company description
- **Permitted** inside Automação — "agentes e IA quando aplicável" (§24)
- **Permitted** inside Software as an implementation capability (§25)
- **Present, but subordinate**, in project narratives — framed by the problem
  solved, not by the technology used (§9.4)

This is the site's largest standing narrative risk (Positioning §15.1), because
both available projects are AI-categorised. The narrative discipline is what
prevents the portfolio from repositioning the company.

## 5. Value narrative

`INTERPRETED` — PRD §5; Positioning §6, §7.

### 5.1 What changes for the customer

The value narrative describes the **after**, and it must do so without metrics,
guarantees or invented results (§53).

| Before | After |
| --- | --- |
| Information re-entered by hand | Systems exchange it without supervision |
| A process that runs if someone remembers | A process that runs |
| Answers assembled from several places | One reliable answer |
| A capability blocked by a technical gap | The capability exists |
| Skilled people doing mechanical work | Skilled people doing work that needs judgement |
| Growth that requires proportional headcount | Volume the operation absorbs |

### 5.2 The value in one sentence

> **O trabalho que não deveria ser feito por pessoas para de ser feito por
> pessoas.**

`DIRECTION` — shows the shape of the value claim. Not copy.

### 5.3 The deeper value

`INTERPRETED` — Positioning §4.2.

Beneath the functional outcome:

> **The problem stops being yours.**

It stops being something the buyer manages, explains and works around. This is
what *Evoluímos* implies (§15) — and it is why the service-model decision
(Positioning §17.3) matters narratively, not just commercially: the narrative
promises continuity that the commercial model has not yet defined.

### 5.4 Hard constraint on value language

Never: percentages, hours saved, ROI, efficiency figures, guarantees, or "até
X%" constructions. These are prohibited (§53, Positioning §13.4) and would
require evidence PXTO does not have.

The value narrative is written in **qualitative, concrete, operational**
language. Concreteness replaces quantification — "a planilha deixa de existir"
is specific without being a measured claim.

## 6. Brand story

`INTERPRETED` — PRD §1.1, §27, §57; Brand Foundation §1, §2, §5.

### 6.1 The constraint that defines the story

PXTO has no publishable history, no founding anecdote cleared for use, no team
to describe and no client list (Positioning §12, §13). The conventional brand
story — *"founded in X, we grew to Y"* — is unavailable, and inventing one is
prohibited (§53).

**Therefore the brand story is not an origin story. It is a story of belief and
stance.**

This is not a compromise. A belief-based story is more durable than an origin
story, and it is fully available on day one.

### 6.2 The structure

**Belief**
> Most companies do not have a technology problem. They have a problem between
> technologies.

**Observation**
> Tools are bought to solve individual problems. The space between them belongs
> to nobody — so the operation absorbs it, in manual work nobody planned for and
> nobody measures.

**Stance**
> That space is worth taking seriously. It is where the operation actually
> happens.

**Practice**
> Understand the problem before proposing anything. Build only what the problem
> requires. Choose the technology for the problem, never the reverse. Say only
> what can be supported.

**Consequence**
> The tools will change. The capability will not.
> (§57 — the PRD's own closing statement.)

### 6.3 Why this story is credible from a new company

It makes no claim requiring history. Every element is verifiable **in the
encounter itself** — in how the site is written, how the problem is described,
and how the first conversation goes (Positioning §10). The reader can check it
while reading it.

### 6.4 What the brand story must never contain

- An origin date, founding narrative or growth story — unless PXTO supplies one
- Any first-person singular. The company speaks (§1.1, §27)
- A founder's personal journey, credentials or history (§17.5, anti-pattern)
- Implied scale: "nossa equipe", "nossos clientes", "nossa trajetória"
- Mission-statement abstraction — "acreditamos em inovação" says nothing

### 6.5 Unresolved

`PROPOSAL` — whether PXTO has an origin story it wishes to tell, and whether the
founder appears at all, are open (Brand Foundation §15.6, §15.10). Until decided,
the story runs on belief and stance alone, which is sufficient.

## 7. Homepage narrative arc

`INTERPRETED` — structure `DERIVED` from PRD §11.2.

The Home page is a single argument, not a set of sections. Each block has one
narrative job and one transition it must earn.

```text
Navbar → Hero → Problema → Posicionamento → Soluções → Como trabalhamos
       → Projetos → Diferenciais → CTA → Footer
```

### 7.1 The arc, block by block

| # | Block | Narrative job | Reader state entering | Reader state leaving |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Hero** | Answer "what is this company" in seconds (§12) | Unknown company, low patience | "A technology company that connects, automates and builds" |
| 2 | **Problema** | Recognition, naming, reframe (§3.2) | Interested but detached | "This is about my company" |
| 3 | **Posicionamento** | Name the space PXTO works in | Problem recognised, no solution shape | "There is a company for this specific gap" |
| 4 | **Soluções** | Show four instruments, one capability (§4.3) | Wants to know what is offered | "They can address my case, whichever it is" |
| 5 | **Como trabalhamos** | Method as evidence; relieve the "I can't brief it" fear (§4.2) | Interested, uncertain how to start | "I don't need a spec. I need to describe the problem" |
| 6 | **Projetos** | Prove capability through real work (§16) | Persuaded in principle, unproven in fact | "They actually build things, and difficult ones" |
| 7 | **Diferenciais** | Answer "why them" without claiming scale | Convinced it is possible; not yet why PXTO | "Their way of working is the reason" |
| 8 | **CTA** | Convert intent into a low-friction first step (§29) | Ready, but cautious about commitment | "I can just describe my problem" |

### 7.2 The transitions — where arcs actually fail

The transition matters more than the block. Each must be earned:

| Transition | What must have happened |
| --- | --- |
| Hero → Problema | The reader knows the category and is willing to spend 20 more seconds |
| Problema → Posicionamento | Recognition landed. **If the reader did not see themselves, the rest of the page is decoration** |
| Posicionamento → Soluções | The reader accepts the gap is a real, addressable thing |
| Soluções → Como trabalhamos | Curiosity has shifted from *what* to *how* |
| Como trabalhamos → Projetos | Method is credible; the reader now wants evidence |
| Projetos → Diferenciais | Capability is proven; the remaining question is "why you" |
| Diferenciais → CTA | The only thing left is starting |

### 7.3 The Diferenciais block — narrative role

`PROPOSAL` — the PRD lists this block (§11.2) but defines no content anywhere.
Content is business information (Brand Foundation §15, Positioning §11).

Its **narrative job** is determinable from the position: it answers "why PXTO"
at the moment the reader has accepted the problem and the capability. Given the
proof constraints, it can only be built from the structural differentiators in
Positioning §11.1 — whole-path coverage, technology-agnosticism, problem-first
method, disciplined restraint, demonstrated technical range.

**It must not become:** a badge row, a stats bar, a logo strip, or a
"why choose us" list of adjectives. Each of those requires proof PXTO does not
have, and §13.6 of Positioning forbids layouts that create pressure to invent it.

### 7.4 The arc's single point of failure

**Block 2.** If Problema does not produce recognition, nothing downstream
recovers — the reader reads the rest as generic vendor material. Everything the
positioning claims about understanding real business problems is won or lost in
that one section.

## 8. Service narrative

`DERIVED` / `INTERPRETED` — PRD §22–§26; Positioning §7.3, §14.3.

### 8.1 The frame

The Soluções page tells one story with four chapters (§4.3). The overview must
establish the shared logic **before** the four appear, otherwise the page reads
as a service menu — anti-pattern §17.1.

Shared logic: *the same diagnosis leads to different builds.*

### 8.2 Per-capability narrative

Positioning lines are `DERIVED` from §23–§26 and are fixed copy in the PRD.

| Capability | Positioning line (§23–§26) | Who arrives here | What they believe | Narrative job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Integrações** | *Faça seus sistemas conversarem.* | Someone whose team reconciles data by hand | "These tools were never meant to connect" | Show the gap is closable, and not tied to specific products (§23) |
| **Automação** | *Automatize o trabalho que não deveria precisar ser manual.* | Someone whose process depends on a person remembering | "Automating this would be complicated" | Show the work is describable, therefore automatable |
| **Software** | *Quando uma solução pronta não basta, construímos a sua.* | Someone stuck at the last 20% of an off-the-shelf tool | "We'll have to adapt to the software" | Legitimise building; keep the focus on the solution, not the stack (§25) |
| **Sites** | *Presença digital construída para funcionar.* | Someone needing a presence that works, not decoration | "A site is a design project" | Reframe as an **operational surface** — the anti-agency move (§26) |

### 8.3 The Sites narrative — special handling

`DERIVED` — §26 explicitly forbids positioning this as a traditional creative
agency offer; Positioning §15.5 identifies it as a commoditisation risk.

Sites is narrated as **the operational edge of the same story**: a surface that
must load fast, be found, be usable and connect to the company's tools —
performance, SEO, integration (§26). Visual craft is a requirement, never the
subject.

Practically: the Sites page argues from function. The site the reader is
currently on is the argument (Positioning §12.1).

### 8.4 Cross-capability rules

1. **No capability may claim primacy.** Order is presentational, not
   hierarchical (§4)
2. **Each solution page opens with the problem, not the offer** (§3.4)
3. **No tool lock-in in the language.** §23 explicitly forbids limiting
   communication to specific tools
4. **Each capability ends by returning to the whole** — the reader should
   understand PXTO can also do the adjacent thing, because the diagnosis decides
5. **Every solution page carries a CTA to its own page from Home** (§14) and a
   contact CTA of its own (§22)

## 9. Project narrative

`DERIVED` / `INTERPRETED` — PRD §16, §19, §20, §21; Positioning §12, §15.6.

### 9.1 What projects are for

Not to show client volume (§16). To demonstrate **capability, reasoning,
execution, technology and the variety of problems solved**.

"Reasoning" is the distinctive one. A reader can evaluate thinking even when
they cannot evaluate code — and thinking is what a new company has in abundance.

### 9.2 The case story shape

Mapped onto the PRD's block structure (§21):

| Narrative beat | PRD block |
| --- | --- |
| **Situation** — the world before | Contexto |
| **Tension** — what did not work | Problema |
| **Constraint** — what made it non-trivial | Desafio |
| **Decision** — the approach chosen, and why | Solução |
| **Build** — how it actually works | Como funciona |
| **Instruments** — what was used | Tecnologia / capacidades |
| **Resolution** — what changed | Resultado |
| **Evidence** — see it | Galeria / demonstração |
| **Invitation** | CTA |

Not every block appears in every project; content is proportional to relevance
and to what may be disclosed (§21).

### 9.3 The narrative rule for every case

> **Every project is a story about a problem. The technology is a supporting
> character.**

A case that opens with the stack has already failed — it repositions PXTO as a
software house (§17.1) or an AI company (§17.3).

### 9.4 Framing the two existing projects

`DERIVED` — §17, §18.

Both are AI-categorised, against a positioning that forbids AI as identity (§4).
The PRD itself already supplies the correct framing in each project's *Objetivo
do case*.

| Project | Narrate as | Not as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Furniture Visualization** (§17) | Letting someone see, concretely, whether a piece of furniture fits a room — solved by combining visual input, processing and image generation | "An AI image-generation app" |
| **AI Interior Designer** (§18) | Turning a photograph of a room into a usable starting point for design decisions | "An AI assistant with computer vision" |

Both demonstrate the same underlying thing: **PXTO turns a concept into a working
product, combining technologies that did not previously fit together.** That is
a statement about capability, not about AI.

**Portfolio composition is a narrative instrument** (Positioning §16.11). Adding
a non-AI case is the single most effective narrative correction available
(Positioning §12.3).

### 9.5 Narrating a restricted project

`INTERPRETED` — from §20, which permits anonymised presentation.

Neither project has disclosure clearance (Positioning §12.1), and future ones may
be restricted. The anonymised pattern:

- Name the **sector or situation**, not the company: "uma empresa que…"
- Lead harder on **problem and reasoning** — neither requires identification
- Describe capability categories where specific technologies are restricted
- Describe the change qualitatively where metrics are not authorised
- **Never** use a placeholder logo, a blurred mark, or a hint at the client's
  identity

A well-told anonymous case is more persuasive than a poorly told named one. The
absence of a name must never read as concealment — treat discretion as a stated
professional standard (Brand Foundation §5.5).

### 9.6 The project index narrative

Title and subtitle are fixed by §16: *O que construímos.* / *Soluções
desenvolvidas para transformar ideias e problemas em produtos digitais
funcionais.*

With few projects, the index must **never** imply that more exist and are hidden,
and must never pad with placeholders. Few cases, told deeply, read as
selectivity. Few cases in a grid built for twenty read as emptiness — a layout
consequence, flagged for design (Positioning §13.6).

## 10. About narrative

`DERIVED` / `INTERPRETED` — PRD §27; Brand Foundation §6, §8.

### 10.1 The job

Build trust (§27). Not to tell a history — PXTO has no publishable one.

### 10.2 The structural rule

> **"A PXTO é…" — never "Eu sou…"** (§27)

Absolute, across every page, profile and channel (Brand Foundation §8, §14.4).

### 10.3 What the page can say without inventing anything

`INTERPRETED` — §27 authorises: why the company exists, how it thinks, how it
works, its principles, its view of technology, its approach, its culture.

| Content | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Why PXTO exists | Brand Foundation §2 |
| What PXTO believes about technology in companies | §6.2, Brand Foundation §1 |
| How PXTO works | §15 method |
| What PXTO will and will not do | Brand Foundation §5 values |
| What PXTO is not | §4 — stated as focus, never as attack on other categories |

That is a complete About page containing **no history, no team, no clients, no
numbers**.

### 10.4 The trap

An About page with nothing to say fills the space with abstraction —
"transformamos negócios através da inovação". That is the failure mode (§7).

**The antidote is specificity about method and belief**, which is available
without any history. A page that says exactly how the company thinks is more
trust-building than one that says it was founded in a given year.

### 10.5 Blocked

`PROPOSAL` — the About page cannot be completed until: the vision (Brand
Foundation §15.1), founder visibility (§15.6), the meaning of the name (§15.10)
and the company model (§15.4) are decided. **Narrative structure can be drafted;
the page cannot be finished.**

## 11. Contact narrative

`DERIVED` / `INTERPRETED` — PRD §28, §29; Positioning §5.4, §15.9.

### 11.1 Reader state on arrival

They have decided PXTO is worth a conversation. They are now weighing exposure:
being sold to, wasting time, having to explain something they cannot articulate.

### 11.2 The narrative job

> Make this feel like **starting a conversation**, not submitting to a sales
> process.

### 11.3 The controlling message

> **Descreva o problema. Não é preciso ter a solução.**

`DIRECTION` — this resolves the secondary tension (§2.3) precisely at the point
of action, and mirrors *Entendemos* (§15). It is the highest-leverage message on
the page.

### 11.4 Narrative rules

1. **Ask for the problem, not the brief.** The form's "O que você precisa?"
   (§28) is orientation, not qualification
2. **Keep it short.** The form must stay simple (§28). Every field is friction
3. **No commitment language.** No "solicite um orçamento", no "agende uma
   demonstração" (§29)
4. **State what happens next** — reduces the fear of the black hole. Requires a
   human decision on the actual response commitment (§18)
5. **Offer direct contact as an alternative** (§28). Some buyers will not use a
   form. Channels are undefined (Architecture, open question)
6. **Every state speaks in the brand voice** — loading, success and error (§39)

### 11.5 After submission

The success state is the last narrative moment on the site and the first of the
relationship. It should confirm receipt and set an expectation — not thank
effusively. What expectation can honestly be set is unresolved (§18).

## 12. Main message

`DERIVED` — PRD §6, §12.

### 12.1 Current default, fixed by the PRD

> **Conectamos sistemas. Automatizamos processos. Construímos soluções.**

Complementary (§6, used as hero subheadline in §12):

> Tecnologia aplicada aos desafios reais das empresas.

§6 states these may be refined during branding/copywriting. Refinement is a
human decision.

### 12.2 A strategic observation worth raising

`INTERPRETED`

The main message is built from **three capability verbs**. In the value-
proposition structure (Positioning §7.2) that is layer 2 — *how PXTO works* —
while the positioning recommends leading with layer 1, *what changes for the
buyer*.

This is a real, though contained, tension. Three factors resolve it:

1. The subheadline carries layer 1 — "desafios reais das empresas" places the
   claim in the buyer's world
2. The Home arc immediately follows the hero with **Problema** (§11.2), so the
   argument self-corrects within one scroll
3. The three verbs give the reader immediate categorical clarity, which a
   new and unknown company needs more than a well-known one does

**Recommendation:** retain the PRD message. Ensure the subheadline and the
Problema section carry the problem-first weight. **Flagged for PXTO's awareness,
not proposed for change.**

### 12.3 The message beneath the message

Internal, never copy:

> PXTO understands the problem before proposing the technology.

Everything in §13 is a facet of this.

## 14. CTA strategy

`DERIVED` — PRD §29; Positioning §2.5, §5.4.

### 14.1 Fixed by the PRD

**Primary, site-wide:** *Fale com a PXTO*
**Contextual alternatives:** *Vamos conversar* · *Conte seu projeto* · *Quero
conversar sobre meu projeto* · *Começar uma conversa*
**Secondary, hero (§12):** *Ver projetos*

**Prohibited:** "Saiba mais" as a primary CTA · "Clique aqui" · "Solicite agora"
where no request process exists.

### 14.2 The strategic principle

Every approved CTA is **conversational, not transactional**. This is not a style
preference — it is the correct response to a buyer who cannot yet specify what
they need (§2.3) and who is risk-averse toward a new vendor (Positioning §15.9).
A transactional CTA asks for a decision the reader is not ready to make.

### 14.3 Commitment ladder

| Level | Action | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Low** | Look at the work — *Ver projetos* | Hero secondary; end of solution sections |
| **Medium** | Go deeper into one capability | Solution cards on Home (§14 requires a CTA per solution) |
| **High** | Start a conversation — *Fale com a PXTO* | Navbar; Home CTA block; end of every page |

The site never forces the jump from arrival to high commitment. Both hero CTAs
(§12) offer the choice explicitly.

### 14.4 Placement by page

| Page | Primary | Secondary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home — Hero | *Fale com a PXTO* | *Ver projetos* (§12) |
| Home — after each solution | Go to that solution page (§14) | — |
| Home — CTA block | *Fale com a PXTO* | — |
| Soluções | *Fale com a PXTO* (§22) | Individual solution pages |
| Solution pages | Contextual — *Conte seu projeto* | Related projects |
| Projetos index | *Fale com a PXTO* | — |
| Project page | Contextual — *Quero conversar sobre meu projeto* (§21) | Other projects |
| Sobre | *Fale com a PXTO* | *Ver projetos* |
| Navbar | *Fale com a PXTO* — highlighted (§10.1) | — |

### 14.5 CTA rules

1. One primary CTA per view. Competing CTAs of equal weight reduce action
2. A CTA follows an argument — never precedes the reason to act
3. Contextual wording matches what the reader just read; the destination stays
   consistent
4. Never manufacture urgency. There is no scarcity, and inventing one violates
   the tone (§7)
5. The CTA is an invitation to describe a problem, not to request a quote

## 15. Messaging hierarchy

`INTERPRETED`

### 15.1 The levels

```text
Level 0 — ESSENCE            Tecnologia aplicada a problemas reais
                             Brand Foundation §1 · internal only
        ↓
Level 1 — POSITION           O espaço entre os sistemas
                             Positioning §14 · internal only
        ↓
Level 2 — MAIN MESSAGE       Conectamos sistemas. Automatizamos processos.
                             Construímos soluções.  (§6) · public
        ↓
Level 3 — SUPPORTING         The seven messages in §13 · public
        ↓
Level 4 — PAGE MESSAGE       One controlling idea per page
        ↓
Level 5 — SECTION MESSAGE    One idea per section
        ↓
Level 6 — COPY               Headlines, body, labels — `../content/`
```

### 15.2 The governing rule

> **No level may contradict the level above it. Every level must be traceable
> upward.**

If a headline cannot be traced to a supporting message, and that message to the
main message, and the main message to the position — the headline is decoration
and should be cut.

### 15.3 Level 4 — one controlling idea per page

`PROPOSAL`

| Page | Controlling idea |
| --- | --- |
| Home | Your systems do not talk, and that is solvable |
| Soluções | Four instruments, one diagnosis |
| Integrações | The gap between your systems can be closed |
| Automação | Work that should not be manual, is not |
| Software | When nothing fits, it can be built |
| Sites | A digital presence that works, not just looks right |
| Projetos | Real problems, actually solved |
| Sobre | How this company thinks |
| Contato | Describe the problem; that is enough to start |

### 15.4 Levels 0 and 1 are internal

The essence and the position are compasses. They are **never printed on the
site**. Printing a positioning statement is the mark of a company that has not
translated it into a story.

## 17. Narrative anti-patterns — the five voices to avoid

`DERIVED` — PRD §4, §26, §33; Brand Foundation §9; plus the constraint stated by
PXTO.

Each anti-pattern has a **tell** — a structural signature, not a word list.

### 17.1 The generic software house
**Tell:** the site talks about *what it delivers* instead of *what the reader
has*. Service lists, technology logos, "desenvolvimento sob demanda", process
diagrams with no problem attached.
**Fix:** every section opens with the reader's situation (§3.4). The offer is an
answer, never an opening.

### 17.2 The traditional web agency
**Tell:** craft and aesthetics as the subject. Portfolio as a gallery.
"Experiências digitais memoráveis". Visual work framed as the value.
**Fix:** Sites is narrated as an operational surface (§8.3). Projects are told as
problems, not as showcases (§9.3). Design carries authority by *being* good, not
by being discussed (§33).

### 17.3 The AI-only company
**Tell:** AI in the hero, the navigation or the company description. Projects
titled by their technology. AI presented as the reason to be interested.
**Fix:** the placement rules in §4.5 and the project framing in §9.4. AI never
appears above the solution-page level.
**Note:** this is the highest-probability failure, because the current portfolio
pulls toward it (Positioning §15.1).

### 17.4 The low-code automation agency
**Tell:** platform names as identity. "Especialistas em [ferramenta]". Workflow
screenshots as proof. Positioning tied to a tool's ecosystem.
**Fix:** §23's rule — never limit communication to specific tools. The
technology is chosen for the problem (§57). Never name a platform as a
credential.

### 17.5 The personal freelancer brand
**Tell:** first person singular. A face on the homepage. Personal credentials,
personal history, a portfolio of "my work". "Olá, eu sou…".
**Fix:** the company speaks (§1.1, §27, §10.2). Absolute across every surface.

### 17.6 The diagnostic

Apply to any page before it ships:

1. Could this text appear on a competitor's site unchanged? → **generic**
2. Does the reader's problem appear before PXTO's offer? → if not, §17.1
3. Is any technology named above solution-page level? → §17.3 or §17.4
4. Is any visual craft claim the subject rather than a requirement? → §17.2
5. Does any first-person singular appear anywhere? → §17.5
6. Does any sentence claim something unsourceable? → §53 violation
7. Can every headline be traced up the hierarchy (§15.2)? → if not, cut it
