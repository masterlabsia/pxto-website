## How to read this document

Every statement carries a status. Do not treat them as equivalent.

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `DERIVED` | Restates a decision already made in the PRD. **Binding.** Cite the section. |
| `INTERPRETED` | Synthesis of several PRD statements into a brand-level formulation. No new business fact introduced. **Should be confirmed by a human**, but is safe to work from. |
| `PROPOSAL` | Not settled by the PRD. **Not approved. Does not bind implementation.** Listed again in §15. |

Nothing in this document asserts a client, a number, a result, a date, a team
size, a certification, an award, a partnership or a piece of company history.
Where such a fact would normally appear, the gap is left explicit (PRD §53).

**Language note.** Explanation is in English. Portuguese formulations — the ones
that will eventually reach the site or a client conversation — are given in
Portuguese and must not be translated. Word lists in §12 and §13 are Portuguese
because the site is Portuguese.

## 1. Brand essence

`INTERPRETED` — from PRD §5, §57.

The essence is the irreducible core of the brand. It is an internal compass, not
a tagline and not copy.

> **Tecnologia aplicada a problemas reais.**

Expanded, for internal use:

PXTO exists at the point where a business problem meets the systems that were
supposed to solve it. The company's core capability is not any one technology —
it is the ability to understand a real operational problem and build whatever is
necessary to resolve it (§57).

The PRD states this as the final directive:

> Entendemos o problema.
> Encontramos a solução.
> Construímos o que for necessário.

Three properties follow, and they govern everything else in this document:

1. **The problem comes first, the technology second.** The company does not sell
   technology for its own sake (§3).
2. **The tools are interchangeable; the capability is not.** "A tecnologia
   utilizada pode mudar. A capacidade central da PXTO permanece" (§57).
3. **The claim never exceeds the evidence.** Credibility is built without
   overstating experience or inventing results (§2.5, §7, §53).

## 2. Brand purpose

`INTERPRETED` — from PRD §3, §5, §8, §57.

Purpose answers why the company exists beyond selling hours.

> PXTO exists so that companies stop losing time, information and capacity to
> systems that do not talk to each other and to work that should never have been
> manual.

The audience the PRD describes is not asking for technology. It is describing
friction (§8.2): *"Preciso integrar dois sistemas." · "Minha equipe perde tempo
fazendo trabalho manual." · "Tenho várias planilhas que deveriam conversar." ·
"Meu sistema atual não atende uma necessidade específica."*

The purpose is to remove that friction — through integration, automation,
software or a website, whichever the problem actually requires.

**Boundary:** this is a statement of intent, not a claim of impact. It says what
PXTO is for, not what PXTO has already achieved. Achievement claims require
evidence supplied by PXTO (§53).

## 3. Mission

`DERIVED` — from PRD §5, restated at brand level.

What PXTO does, continuously, today:

> **Transformar necessidades reais de negócio em soluções digitais — conectando
> sistemas, automatizando processos e desenvolvendo software sob medida.**

This is the PRD's value proposition (§5) treated as the mission, because the PRD
frames it as an ongoing activity rather than a future state. The four capability
areas through which the mission is executed (§1.1, §14):

| Area | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Integrações** | Connect systems, APIs and tools so information moves automatically between platforms |
| **Automação** | Turn repetitive processes into automated flows, reducing manual work |
| **Software** | Build applications and systems for specific business needs |
| **Sites** | Build modern, fast, responsive sites for companies establishing or strengthening a digital presence |

Descriptions above are the PRD's own (§14).

## 4. Vision

`PROPOSAL — NOT APPROVED. Requires a decision by PXTO.`

**The PRD does not contain a vision.** A vision is a claim about where the
company intends to go — its horizon, ambition, scale and scope. That is
information only the company's owners hold, and it cannot be derived from a
website requirements document without inventing it.

What the PRD does establish, and what any vision must remain consistent with:

- The brand is built to outlast its current technology stack (§57).
- The website is meant to serve as an **institutional base for the company's
  future initiatives** (§2.7) — implying PXTO anticipates activity beyond
  client services, though the PRD does not say what.
- The brand is corporate and independent of its founders (§1.1), which implies
  an entity intended to persist beyond the people currently in it.
- No numeric targets are to be set before sufficient history exists (§51).

**A proposed direction, for discussion only — not to be used anywhere:**

> Ser a empresa que empresas procuram quando o problema é real, específico e
> ninguém tem uma solução pronta.

This is offered as a starting point for a conversation, not as a decision. It
must be accepted, rewritten or rejected by PXTO before any use. Until then,
**the website must not state a vision.** Its absence is not a defect; a
fabricated one would be.

See §15.1 for what is needed to settle this.

## 5. Values

`INTERPRETED` — from PRD §7, §32, §52, §53, §57. Behavioural rules already
mandated by the PRD, expressed as values.

These are not aspirational adjectives. Each one is enforceable, and each already
has teeth somewhere in the project.

### 5.1 Evidence over claim
The company says only what it can support. No overstated experience, no invented
client count, no unproven promise, no superlative without evidence (§7, §53).
*Enforced by:* the content rules in §53 and the project disclosure checklist in
§20.

### 5.2 The problem before the solution
Work starts by understanding the problem, its context and its objective — not by
choosing a technology (§15, §3). Technology is a means (§5).
*Enforced by:* the process — Entendemos precedes Construímos (§15).

### 5.3 Clarity over sophistication
Communication is clear, direct and objective. Buzzwords, empty corporate
language and unnecessary technical vocabulary are treated as failures, not
style (§7).
*Enforced by:* §13 of this document.

### 5.4 Build what is necessary — no more
The simplest option consistent with the existing architecture wins. No effects
to demonstrate technical ability, no performance traded for aesthetics, no
duplicate components without justification (§32, §52).
*Enforced by:* the design rules (§32) and the AI ambiguity protocol (§52).

### 5.5 Discretion
Client names, logos, internal data, proprietary architecture, commercial
information and unauthorised metrics are not published without authorisation. A
project can be told anonymously (§20).
*Enforced by:* the publication criteria (§20).

### 5.6 Durability
Solutions and the brand are built to be maintained and evolved, not rebuilt.
Technology may change; the capability remains (§50.15, §57).
*Enforced by:* the acceptance criterion that the site can evolve without
architectural rebuild (§50.15).

> **Open question:** these six are derived from how the PRD requires the company
> to behave. Whether they are the values PXTO would independently state about
> itself is a question for PXTO (§15.2).

## 6. Brand personality

`INTERPRETED` — from PRD §7, §33.

If PXTO were a person in a room with a client, it would be **the engineer who
asks the right question before proposing anything, and whose answer you trust
because it is never inflated.**

| Trait | Expressed as | Never becomes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Precise** | Says exactly what it means; specific over general | Pedantic, cold |
| **Confident** | States capability without hedging | Arrogant, boastful |
| **Direct** | Gets to the point; no throat-clearing | Blunt, dismissive |
| **Technical** | Understands the substance | Jargon-heavy, exclusionary |
| **Pragmatic** | Oriented to what works and what solves | Cynical, unambitious |
| **Human** | Talks to a person, not a market segment | Casual, jokey, familiar |
| **Sober** | Calm, understated, unhurried | Dull, corporate, faceless |

The PRD's own adjective set (§7): clara, direta, profissional, moderna, técnica
sem ser excessivamente técnica, confiante, objetiva, orientada a problemas,
orientada a soluções, humana.

The visual counterpart (§33): precisão, tecnologia, engenharia, simplicidade,
sofisticação, confiança.

**The single most important calibration:** confident but never inflated. The
brand's credibility comes from restraint. A company that resists overclaiming
reads as one that does not need to.

## 7. Desired brand perception

`DERIVED` / `INTERPRETED` — from PRD §2.5, §4, §33, §57.

After a first encounter with PXTO, the intended impression is:

1. **"This is a serious technology company."** Not a freelancer, not a template,
   not a side project. The design itself must carry this (§33).
2. **"They understand problems like mine."** Recognition before persuasion — the
   visitor sees their own situation described (§13).
3. **"They could probably build what I need."** Capability demonstrated through
   real work, not asserted (§2.4, §16).
4. **"They are not overselling."** Credibility built without exaggeration
   (§2.5).
5. **"I know how to start a conversation."** The path to contact is obvious
   (§2.6, §3).

Perceptions to actively avoid:

- a generic AI startup (§33) — see §14.3;
- a creative agency, a SaaS template, a low-code shop (§4, §33);
- a personal brand or one person's portfolio (§1.1, §27);
- a vendor selling technology rather than solving problems (§3, §57).

## 8. What PXTO is

`DERIVED` — from PRD §1.1, §4, §5, §14.

- A **technology company**, presented as an **independent corporate brand** tied
  to the company (CNPJ) — **not** to the personal identity of its founders or
  collaborators (§1.1).
- A company operating **at the intersection of technology and operations**,
  turning real business problems into digital solutions (§1.1).
- A company that **connects systems, automates processes and builds digital
  solutions** (§4).
- A company with four capability areas: **integrações, automação, software,
  sites** (§1.1, §14).
- A company for which **technology is the means and business results are the
  end** (§5).

**On the founder.** The brand speaks as an entity: *"A PXTO é…"*, never *"Eu
sou…"* (§27). This is a structural rule, not a stylistic preference. It applies
to every page, every project description, every bio, every profile and every
external channel. Individual expertise may exist behind the work; it is not the
thing being sold.

## 9. What PXTO is not

`DERIVED` — from PRD §4, §26, §33, plus the AI constraint.

The PRD is explicit: these activities are part of the company's capability, but
**none of them may define the brand on its own** (§4).

PXTO is **not**, and must not be presented exclusively as:

- an **agency** (§4);
- a **website developer** (§4);
- a **consultancy** (§4);
- an **AI company** (§4) — see §14.3;
- a **low-code automation company** (§4);
- a **traditional software house** (§4).

Further:

- Not a **traditional creative visual agency** — the Sites offer must not be
  positioned this way (§26).
- Not a **personal brand** — see §8 (§1.1, §27).
- Not a **product company** — the PRD describes bespoke solutions for client
  needs (§5, §25); if this changes, it is a positioning decision (§15.4).
- Not a **reseller or implementer of a specific platform** — messaging must not
  be limited to specific tools (§23).

The failure mode this list guards against: any single capability becoming the
brand's identity because it happens to be the most recent work, the most
fashionable, or the easiest to explain.

## 10. Tone of voice

`DERIVED` — from PRD §7, §29.

### Core register
Clear · direct · professional · modern · technical without being excessively
technical · confident · objective · problem-oriented · solution-oriented · human
(§7).

### Rules

1. **Lead with the problem, not the capability.** The reader recognises their
   situation before they are told what PXTO sells (§13).
2. **Short sentences. Concrete nouns.** If a sentence survives deletion of half
   its words, delete them.
3. **Speak to a person.** Second person, professional register. Not "as
   empresas" in the abstract when "sua empresa" is meant (§8).
4. **Never claim what cannot be shown.** No implied client volume, no implied
   years of operation, no implied scale (§7, §53).
5. **Explain, do not impress.** Technical terms are used when they are the
   accurate word, not to signal competence (§7).
6. **No filler CTA language.** Primary CTA is *Fale com a PXTO*. Avoid "Saiba
   mais" as a primary CTA, "Clique aqui", and "Solicite agora" where no request
   process exists (§29).
7. **Consistency across surfaces.** The tone does not change between the
   website, a proposal, an e-mail and a social profile.

### Calibration test
Before publishing any sentence, ask:

- Could a competitor's website say this exact sentence? → it is too generic.
- Does it claim something PXTO cannot evidence? → cut it (§53).
- Would a senior operations director find it obvious, or insulting? → adjust.
- Does it sound like a person, or like a brochure? → rewrite.

## 11. Communication principles

`DERIVED` — from PRD §3, §7, §13, §16, §53, §57.

1. **Business problem first, technology second.** Communication is oriented to
   business problems, operational efficiency and digital solutions — never to
   technology for its own sake (§3).
2. **Recognition before persuasion.** The visitor should see their own situation
   described in concrete, recognisable terms before any pitch (§13).
3. **Demonstrate, do not assert.** Capability is shown through real projects —
   reasoning, execution, variety of problems solved — not through adjectives.
   The goal is never to demonstrate client quantity (§16).
4. **Proportion to evidence.** Every factual claim traces to information
   supplied by PXTO. Where information does not exist, the claim does not exist
   (§53).
5. **The four capabilities are peers.** No single area is presented as the
   company's identity; each is a route into the same core capability (§4, §57).
6. **Restraint is a signal.** Understatement reads as confidence in this
   category; inflation reads as inexperience (§7).
7. **Discretion by default.** Nothing about a client is published without
   authorisation; an anonymised case is preferable to an unauthorised one (§20).
8. **One voice.** The company speaks, not an individual (§1.1, §27).

## 13. Words and expressions to avoid

### 13.1 Prohibited by the PRD
`DERIVED` — §7, verbatim.

> **"Revolucionário" · "Disruptivo" · "A melhor solução" · "Transformação
> digital"** (as an empty phrase)

Also prohibited as categories (§7):

- excessive buzzwords;
- empty corporate language;
- excessive technical terms;
- unproven promises;
- superlatives without evidence;
- **any exaggeration of experience or number of clients.**

### 13.2 Claims that cannot be made
`DERIVED` — §53. These are not stylistic preferences; they are factual
prohibitions.

Never state or imply: clients · numbers · results · testimonials · logos ·
certifications · partners · awards · metrics · technologies used in a project
without confirmation.

This includes **implied** claims — "empresas de todo o Brasil", "anos de
experiência", "centenas de processos automatizados", "confiada por" — where no
supplied evidence exists.

### 13.3 Extensions
`INTERPRETED` — consistent with §7 and §33, added for practical use.

| Avoid | Why |
| --- | --- |
| *inovador · exclusivo · líder · referência · pioneiro* | Superlatives without evidence (§7) |
| *soluções de ponta · tecnologia de última geração* | Empty corporate language (§7) |
| *nossos clientes · nossa equipe · nosso time* | Implies scale and volume not evidenced (§7, §53) |
| *empoderar · alavancar · potencializar · maximizar* | Buzzwords (§7) |
| *end-to-end · full-service · 360°* | Agency vocabulary; PXTO is not an agency (§4) |
| *IA-powered · movido a IA · inteligência artificial* as headline identity | Would position PXTO as an AI company (§4) — see §14.3 |
| *futuro · o amanhã · próxima era* | Conflicts with the "not futuristic" visual and verbal direction (§33) |
| *simples · fácil · rápido* as promises about the client's problem | Trivialises problems the audience finds hard (§8.2, §13) |
| *Saiba mais* as a primary CTA · *Clique aqui* · *Solicite agora* | Prohibited CTA language (§29) |

## 14. Brand positioning implications

`INTERPRETED` — consequences of §§1–13 for how the brand is built and used.

### 14.1 The four capabilities must stay balanced
No solution area may visually or narratively dominate. If one is treated as the
headline offer, the brand collapses into that category and violates §4. The
Soluções page presents all four as peers (§22); the Home presents all four
(§14). This is a layout constraint as much as a copy constraint.

### 14.2 Proof carries the brand, not adjectives
The company has no permission to claim scale, history or results (§53). What
remains is demonstrated capability — the projects section (§16). This makes the
project system the **structurally load-bearing part of the brand**, not a
supporting section. Its quality determines whether the positioning is credible.

**Consequence:** the projects work in `../projects/` is on the brand's critical
path, not a later phase.

### 14.3 AI is a capability, not the positioning
The PRD is explicit that PXTO is not to be presented as an AI company (§4), and
lists AI alongside integrations, automations, software and sites as **tools
inside the proposition** (§57). AI appears in the offer only where the PRD puts
it: *"agentes e IA quando aplicável"* within Automação (§24), and as an
implementation capability within Software (§25).

**A real tension exists.** Both currently documented projects — Furniture
Visualization (§17) and AI Interior Designer (§18) — are AI-categorised. If the
projects section is the primary proof of capability (§14.2), a visitor reading
only the portfolio will conclude PXTO is an AI company, which contradicts §4.

**Handling, unless PXTO decides otherwise:**

- Frame each project by the **problem it solved** and the capability it
  demonstrates, not by its technology label. The PRD already frames both this
  way (§17 "Objetivo do case", §18 "Objetivo do case").
- Present AI as one of several technologies applied, never as the reason the
  project is interesting.
- Prioritise adding at least one integration or automation project to the
  portfolio, so the proof matches the positioning.
- Do not use AI vocabulary in the hero, the navigation, the meta description or
  the company description.

This is flagged as an unresolved strategic decision (§15.5).

### 14.4 The brand must survive the founder
Because the brand is corporate and not personal (§1.1, §27), every artefact —
site, proposals, profiles, e-mail signatures, social accounts — speaks as PXTO.
Anything currently attached to a personal identity is a migration task, not a
copy choice.

### 14.5 Restraint is the differentiator, and it is fragile
In a category crowded with inflated claims, the decision not to overclaim (§7)
is itself the distinguishing signal. It only works if it is total. A single
unsupported superlative undermines the whole position, because it invites the
reader to discount everything else.

### 14.6 The visual identity must carry weight the copy cannot
Since the copy is deliberately restrained and makes no claims of scale, the
**design must supply the credibility** — "o design deve parecer construído por
uma empresa de tecnologia séria" (§33). Under-designing the site is a
positioning failure, not merely an aesthetic one. Equally, over-designing —
gradients, glassmorphism, futuristic effects — reads as the generic AI startup
the brand must not resemble (§33).

### 14.7 The offer is problem-shaped, not service-shaped
The audience arrives describing friction, not requesting a service category
(§8.2). Entry points into the site should map to problems ("meus sistemas não
conversam"), with the four capabilities as the answer — not as a menu the
visitor must self-diagnose against.

## 15. Unresolved strategic decisions

These require a human decision at PXTO. An AI agent must not resolve any of them
(PRD §52). Until each is settled, the corresponding content must not be written.

### 15.1 Vision — **blocking for the Sobre page**
The PRD contains no vision. To settle it, PXTO needs to answer:

- What does PXTO want to be in three to five years — a services company, a
  product company, or both?
- Is growth in team size an objective, or is the model deliberately small?
- What are the "future initiatives" the website is meant to serve as a base for
  (§2.7)? Products? Sub-brands? A platform?
- Is there a target market, sector or company size, or is the positioning
  deliberately horizontal?

### 15.2 Values — confirmation required
The six values in §5 are derived from behaviour the PRD mandates. PXTO must
confirm they are the values the company would state about itself, and whether
any value not visible in a website PRD is missing.

### 15.3 Brand essence and purpose — confirmation required
§1 and §2 are interpretations. They are safe to work from, but they are the
foundation of everything downstream and should be explicitly accepted, adjusted
or rejected before copywriting begins.

### 15.4 Company model — services only, or products too?
The PRD describes bespoke work for clients (§5, §25). §2.7's "future
initiatives" hints at more. This changes the positioning, the Sobre page and
potentially the site architecture. Unresolved.

### 15.5 AI's weight in the portfolio and the positioning
Both documented projects are AI projects, while §4 forbids AI positioning. PXTO
must decide: hold the balanced positioning and add non-AI proof, or
deliberately shift the positioning — which would require amending the PRD.
See §14.3.

### 15.6 Founder visibility
The brand speaks as a company (§1.1, §27). The PRD does not say whether the
founder appears **at all** — a named signature, a LinkedIn link, a face on the
Sobre page. Zero personal presence and a corporate-first presence with a named
person behind it are different decisions. Unresolved.

### 15.7 Target market definition
§8 defines audience by role and situation, not by sector, company size,
geography or ticket. Whether PXTO wants to narrow this is a strategic choice
that would sharpen the messaging considerably. Unresolved.

### 15.8 Category framing
§4 says what PXTO is not, but does not name the category PXTO occupies. If a
visitor must file PXTO somewhere mentally, what is that category? Leaving it
unnamed is a valid choice, but it should be a deliberate one.

### 15.9 Portuguese-only, or bilingual?
The PRD is entirely in Portuguese with Portuguese routes and never mentions
another language. If international work is intended, it affects the brand voice,
the naming and the site architecture. Unresolved.

### 15.10 The name
The PRD never explains what PXTO means, whether it is an acronym, how it is
pronounced, or whether the story behind it is usable. This matters for the Sobre
page and for how the brand introduces itself. Unresolved.
